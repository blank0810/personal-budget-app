Budget Envelopes

What they are, what's broken, and what we're fixing — in pictures.

1

An envelope is a note, not a jar

In some apps, you literally move money into an envelope. Not here. Here an envelope is just a sticky note that says what you plan to spend. The money stays in your account the whole time.

This matters: you can't "run out" of an envelope. You can only go past your own note.

2

The big problem: lost receipts

Every time you log a purchase, there's a little dropdown: "which envelope is this?" If you skip it, the app doesn't count it.

The dangerous part

The mistake only ever goes one way.
Your envelope always looks healthier than real life — never worse.

3

The fix: a "how sure are we?" score

We add one number: coverage. It answers "of all your grocery spending, how much did we actually count?"

New rule

The app may never say "on track" while coverage is below 100%.

And when there's only one obvious envelope for that category, we just tag it for you automatically. No dropdown, no homework. When there are two or more, we ask — because guessing would be a lie.

4

"40% spent" means nothing without a calendar

Right now the app shows the same bar whether it's the 3rd of the month or the 28th. Same picture. Totally different situation.

The app already knows the date and already knows the spend. It just never compared them.

5

The one number you actually want

Not "62% used." Not "₱3,040 remaining." You want to know what you may spend today.

Even better: show it while you're typing the expense, before you hit save. That's the only moment the number can still change what you do.

6

What if you never set a budget?

Most people won't. That's fine — it's not a failure to fix. But today the app basically shrugs at them.

Why we wait

Other apps guess your budget from your bank history on day one. We have no bank connection — so on day one we'd just be asking you to guess. Guessed budgets break immediately and people quit.

So: track quietly for ~6 weeks, then offer. "You've averaged ₱7,800/mo on Groceries. Want to start there?"
